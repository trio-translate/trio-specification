## Fair comparison protocol

Select a concrete situation, exact language directions, dialect/corpus, device/OS, accessory, route, network and power conditions. Record app/firmware version, product tier, setup/downloads, region and test date. Use the same meaningful task and an appropriately matched environment; label unavoidable differences. Separate vendor claims, independently observed behavior, inference and unknowns in every result.

Measure successful understanding/repair, harmful meaning errors, fresh-turn latency, manual actions, recovery, offline startup and full pipeline, music/TTS behavior, microphone truthfulness, accessibility, session duration, privacy choices, and total customer/operating cost. Preserve raw failures and recordings under consent; do not publish a score for a case not run. Use [benchmark procedures](../../harness/benchmarks.md).

## Positioning hypothesis

Trio may differentiate through dependable long conversations with little interaction, explicit offline support for selected configurations, honest recipient-level recovery, and correction/understanding UX. This is a hypothesis to test against mature alternatives, not an established advantage. A longer feature list alone does not justify another app or a subscription.

The documented alternatives already overlap significantly with the vision, including offline processing and background conversation features. The commercial test is whether Trio delivers a meaningfully better complete experience for a specific underserved audience/pair/device combination, at a viable cost. Treat that conclusion as an inference from the documentation and a research question, not proof that a profitable gap exists.

## Maintenance and claim ledger

For each future claim store claim ID, exact wording, product/version, source URL and checked date, method, scope, limitations, reviewer, and refresh trigger. Keep historical observations; do not silently rewrite old results after a vendor changes behavior. Recheck before publishing comparisons, choosing a vendor, setting prices or renewing a supported-capability claim. Proposed recurring review is part of the maintenance workflow, not an automatically scheduled monitor.

Next research priorities: first-release pair/device/market, actual paid tiers and hardware cost, offline hands-on tests, twelve-hour behavior, and customer preference/willingness to pay. No winner or price recommendation is justified by this initial desk research.
